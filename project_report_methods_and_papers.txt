ACTIVE V1 METHODS AND MODELS, WITH WHY THEY WERE USED

1. Spatial artifact encoder
- What was used:
  EfficientNet-B0 on RGB face images.
- Where in code:
  models/spatial_encoder.py
  models/detector.py
- Why it was used:
  EfficientNet-B0 gives a strong accuracy-to-compute tradeoff, which matches the project goal of running on an RTX 4050 with 6 GB VRAM.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We used a pretrained EfficientNet-B0 spatial encoder to learn visual forgery artifacts such as blending inconsistencies, texture distortions, and local appearance anomalies from face crops."

2. Frequency-domain encoder
- What was used:
  A second EfficientNet-B0 processes DCT-transformed input.
- Where in code:
  models/frequency_encoder.py
  datasets/transforms.py
- Why it was used:
  Deepfake generation and compression often leave detectable traces in the frequency domain even when RGB artifacts are visually subtle.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We used a DCT-based frequency branch to capture compression and synthesis artifacts that may not be obvious in the spatial domain."

3. DCT preprocessing
- What was used:
  Block-wise 8x8 DCT on YCrCb channels, followed by normalization and resizing.
- Where in code:
  datasets/transforms.py
  datasets/base_dataset.py
  datasets/kaggle_realfake.py
- Why it was used:
  DCT is a standard way to expose frequency irregularities linked to manipulated or heavily compressed images.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We converted face images into frequency coefficients using an 8x8 block DCT to explicitly model frequency artifacts."

4. Temporal modeling
- What was used:
  Video Swin Transformer Tiny.
- Where in code:
  models/temporal_model.py
  models/detector.py
- Why it was used:
  Deepfake videos can show frame-to-frame inconsistencies in expression, rendering, or motion that are harder to detect from a single image.
- Important note for report:
  The temporal path is implemented, but the current stored inference mode is image mode.
- Report-ready wording:
  "For video analysis, the architecture includes a Video Swin Transformer Tiny to model temporal inconsistency across sampled frames."

5. Physiological consistency modeling
- What was used:
  A BiLSTM over a simple green-channel PPG-like signal extracted from video frames.
- Where in code:
  models/physiology_encoder.py
- Why it was used:
  Real videos contain weak biological rhythms and temporal color variations that can be disrupted by synthetic generation.
- Important note for report:
  This is part of the architecture, but it matters only for video mode.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We included a lightweight physiological encoder that analyzes green-channel temporal variations as a proxy for biological consistency."

6. CLIP alignment module
- What was used:
  Frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 visual encoder plus a learnable projection/alignment head.
- Where in code:
  models/clip_alignment.py
  models/detector.py
- Why it was used:
  The goal is better generalization to unseen generators by aligning learned detector features with broader pretrained visual representations.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We aligned detector features with a frozen CLIP visual encoder to improve transferability and robustness to unseen deepfake generators."

7. Cross-attention multimodal fusion
- What was used:
  Cross-attention between spatial and frequency features, then fusion with temporal features, plus concatenation of physiology and CLIP features.
- Where in code:
  models/fusion.py
- Why it was used:
  Different modalities capture different forgery signals, so fusion lets the model combine complementary evidence instead of relying on a single branch.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We fused multimodal evidence using cross-attention so that spatial and frequency cues could inform each other before final decision making."

8. Multi-task detection head
- What was used:
  One shared head outputs:
  binary real/fake score,
  manipulation-type prediction,
  confidence score.
- Where in code:
  models/detection_head.py
- Why it was used:
  Multi-task learning can encourage richer feature learning and provides more informative outputs than only a binary label.
- Report-ready wording:
  "The prediction head jointly performs binary detection, manipulation-type classification, and confidence estimation."

9. Multi-task training loss
- What was used:
  BCE for real/fake,
  cross-entropy for manipulation type,
  CLIP alignment loss,
  confidence consistency loss.
- Where in code:
  training/losses.py
- Why it was used:
  This encourages the model to optimize detection accuracy while also learning manipulation-aware and representation-aware features.
- Report-ready wording:
  "Training used a multi-component objective combining binary classification loss, manipulation-type loss, CLIP alignment loss, and confidence regularization."

10. Face extraction and preprocessing
- What was used:
  MTCNN-based face detection where available, otherwise resize fallback.
- Where in code:
  datasets/base_dataset.py
- Why it was used:
  Cropping to the face region helps the model focus on the area where most facial manipulations occur.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We used face-centered preprocessing to reduce background noise and focus the detector on manipulation-relevant facial regions."

11. Data augmentation
- What was used:
  resize, horizontal flip, small rotation, JPEG compression, Gaussian blur, Gaussian noise, color jitter, coarse dropout, normalization.
- Where in code:
  datasets/transforms.py
- Why it was used:
  To improve robustness to real-world perturbations and avoid overfitting to clean training conditions.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We applied compression, blur, noise, geometric, and photometric augmentations to improve robustness under realistic image degradations."

12. Training strategy
- What was used:
  AdamW optimizer,
  cosine LR scheduling,
  warmup,
  gradient accumulation,
  AMP mixed precision,
  gradient checkpointing,
  early stopping.
- Where in code:
  training/trainer.py
  configs/config.yaml
- Why it was used:
  To make multimodal training stable and feasible on limited GPU memory.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We used memory-aware training with AdamW, mixed precision, gradient accumulation, and checkpointing to fit the model on limited hardware."

13. Probability calibration
- What was used:
  Temperature scaling on the binary logit and a calibrated decision threshold.
- Where in code:
  calibration.py
  checkpoints/kaggle_realfake/calibration.json
- Why it was used:
  Raw neural probabilities are often miscalibrated, so calibration gives more reliable confidence scores and a better threshold than the default 0.5.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We applied post-hoc temperature scaling and threshold tuning to improve probability calibration and decision reliability."

14. Explainability
- What was used:
  GradCAM heatmaps and structured forensic report generation.
- Where in code:
  explainability/gradcam.py
  explainability/forensic_report.py
- Why it was used:
  To make predictions more interpretable and to visualize which facial regions influenced the decision.
- Report-ready wording:
  "We added explainability through GradCAM-based artifact localization and report generation for more transparent predictions."
